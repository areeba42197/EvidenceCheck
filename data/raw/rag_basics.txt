Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) combines a language model with a search step. Before answering, the system retrieves passages from a document collection and gives them to the model as context.
RAG can reduce unsupported statements because the model is instructed to answer from the retrieved passages. It does not eliminate hallucination: the model can still add details that the passages do not contain.
Retrieval quality matters. If the retriever returns irrelevant passages, the answer may be wrong or unsupported even when the model follows its instructions.
A vector index such as FAISS stores embeddings of text chunks and returns the chunks closest to a query embedding.
